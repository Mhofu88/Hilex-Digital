Digital Assets

A digital asset is something represented, stored or transferred digitally that can carry value, rights or utility. Blockchain-based assets are one category within the wider digital-asset world.

1. Common types of blockchain-based digital assets

Cryptocurrency
A native digital asset used within a blockchain network.
Token
A digital unit created on top of an existing blockchain or platform.
Stablecoin
A token designed to track a reference value such as a national currency, though its stability depends on its design and reserves.
Tokenised asset
A digital representation of a right or claim connected to another asset or service.

2. Utility versus market price

An asset can have a use inside an ecosystem — for payments, access, governance, rewards or services — while also having a market price. These are not the same thing. Market prices can rise or fall rapidly and may be driven by speculation, liquidity and sentiment.

Practical example: a token may be accepted inside an application to pay for a service. That utility does not automatically prove what the token should be worth in national currency.

3. Custodial and self-custodial ownership

On a custodial platform, a company controls the private keys on behalf of the user. In self-custody, the user controls the wallet credentials. Self-custody provides greater control but also gives the user greater responsibility for security and recovery.

4. Important risks

Digital assets can involve price volatility, scams, software vulnerabilities, regulatory restrictions, loss of wallet credentials, platform failures and misleading claims. Never treat promotional language as proof of value or legality.

Key idea: Separate three questions: What does the asset do? Who controls it? What evidence supports claims about its value?

Knowledge Check

1. Is every digital asset a cryptocurrency?

Answer: No. Digital assets are a broader category.

2. Does utility guarantee a rising market price?

Answer: No. Utility and market valuation are different.

3. Who carries more direct key-management responsibility in self-custody?

Answer: The wallet owner.
